## 🎯 The Problem

South African trade businesses — carpenters, joiners, furniture makers — are exceptional at their craft and invisible online. Most operate on word-of-mouth alone, which caps their growth at the size of their immediate network. The challenge: build a web presence that does the trust-building work of a personal referral, at digital scale, for a carpentry business with genuine craft to show.

---

## 💡 The Solution

A portfolio-forward website designed to put the work front and centre. Not a corporate brochure — a craftsperson's showcase. The site architecture answers the three questions every potential client asks: *Can they do what I need? Have they done it before? How do I reach them?* Every design decision flows from those three questions.

---

## 📐 Design Decisions

**Why showcase over sales?** In the trades market, the work sells itself — if you can see it. The design puts the portfolio above the fold, not the pricing or the pitch. Trust comes before transaction.

**Why WhatsApp integration?** South African clients in the trades market communicate via WhatsApp as a default. A phone number alone loses leads. A WhatsApp CTA converts them.

**Why static HTML?** No CMS overhead, no security vulnerabilities, no hosting costs for a client who needs low-maintenance reliability. The site should work flawlessly 3 years from now without intervention.

## 🌱 Build Journey

The trades market is underserved by good web design — most carpentry sites online look like they were built in 2009. That gap is an opportunity. The brief here wasn't to build something complex; it was to build something that looked serious enough to charge serious prices.

The biggest design insight: **photography is the product**. Every layout decision was made in service of displaying the client's work beautifully. Mobile responsiveness on image-heavy sites required careful attention to aspect ratios, lazy loading, and touch-friendly gallery interactions.

This project seeded a conviction about SME digital work in South Africa: trades businesses with genuine quality are leaving significant revenue on the table purely because their digital presence doesn't reflect their standard of work. That gap is a market.
