## 1. The Nseenene (Grasshopper) Clan — Buganda Kingdom, Uganda

The clan the family names first. Nseenene = the edible bush-cricket / long-horned grasshopper,
a delicacy and a seasonal harvest in Buganda.

### Verified

- **Nseenene is a recognised clan (ekika) of the Buganda Kingdom**, with the grasshopper as its
  primary totem (omuziro). The clan's secondary totem (akabbiro) is recorded as **Nabangogoma**
  (and in a second branch, **Kanene**).
  Sources: [Buganda Kingdom official — Nseenene Clan](https://buganda.or.ug/clan/nseenene-clan/),
  [africanculture1 — Nsenene/Grasshopper Clan](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html)

- **Clan head (owakasolya): Mugalula.** The clan's principal seats are at **Kisozi in Ggomba** and
  **Lweza in Busujju**.
  Sources: [buganda.or.ug](https://buganda.or.ug/clan/nseenene-clan/),
  [africanculture1](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html)

- **The family motto is a genuine, documented Nseenene clan motto (omubala).** The version the
  family carries — *"Ggwe Mpagi, ggwe Luwaga; Nakimera muka Ssuuna"* — matches the recorded clan
  mottoes almost verbatim. The full documented set reads:
  > *"(i) Byaja ne bigwa. (ii) Ggwe mpagi, ggwe luwaga. (iii) Nakimera muka Ssuuna bw'asa bwanegula."*
  So "Ggwe Mpagi, ggwe Luwaga" ("You are the pillar, you are the support-beam") and "Nakimera muka
  Ssuuna" are authentic Nseenene clan slogans, not invented.
  Sources: [africanculture1](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html),
  [buganda.or.ug](https://buganda.or.ug/clan/nseenene-clan/)

- **Founding ancestry.** Clan tradition traces the Nseenene to **Kiroboozi**, a herdsman on the
  slopes of Mugamba Hill in Busongola (Tooro region). His children — **Buyonga**, **Kalibbala**,
  and their sister **Nnandawula** — migrated with their cattle through Bwera in Buddu into Ggomba.
  (Note the ancestral sister's name **Nnandawula** — the same root as "Nandawula.")
  Sources: [buganda.or.ug](https://buganda.or.ug/clan/nseenene-clan/),
  [africanculture1](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html)

- **Royal blood-tie to the throne of Buganda.** By clan tradition, Buyonga's daughter **Wannyana**
  married **Omukama Winyi of Bunyoro** and bore **Prince Kimera**, who became the **third Kabaka
  (king) of Buganda** (reign traditionally dated c. 1374–1404). Kimera is independently recorded as
  having grown up at Kibulala "with eleven clans including the Nseenene." The Nseenene are therefore
  remembered as a clan bound to the origins of Buganda kingship — which is why the motto invokes
  *"Nakimera"* (of/from Kimera).
  Sources: [africanculture1](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html),
  [Kimera of Buganda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimera_of_Buganda),
  [Buganda Royal Family — Kabaka Kimera](https://bugandaroyalfamily.wordpress.com/kabaka-kimera/)

- **The clan system (ekika) in Buganda.** A clan is "a group of people who share the same ancestor
  in the distant past" — an extended family whose members treat one another as siblings. Descent is
  strictly patrilineal. Every Muganda belongs to a clan; eating your own totem is taboo. Buganda
  recognises **46 clans today** (some counts cite 52+), descending from an original five founding
  clans (Ffumbe, Lugave, Ngonge, Njaza, Nyonyi). The clan hierarchy runs: **owakasolya** (clan head)
  → **ssiga** → **mutuba** → **lunyiriri** → **nnyumba/enju** (individual house).
  Sources: [BeingAfrican — Clans and Totems in Buganda Culture](https://beingafrican.org/culture-panel/clans-and-totems-in-buganda-culture/),
  [Clans of Baganda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clans_of_Baganda)

**Evocative, sourced details for storytelling:**
1. The grasshopper (nsenene) is not only a totem but a *seasonal harvest* — twice a year, with the
   rains, the swarms come, and in Buganda they are gathered, a delicacy tied to abundance and season.
   (Totem = grasshopper; [buganda.or.ug](https://buganda.or.ug/clan/nseenene-clan/))
2. The clan's own praise calls its people *the pillar and the support-beam* — "Ggwe mpagi, ggwe
   luwaga" — an image of the ones who hold the house up. ([africanculture1](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html))
3. The clan sits inside the founding story of a kingdom: through **Wannyana**, mother of **Kabaka
   Kimera**, the Nseenene are woven into the origins of the Buganda throne. ([Kimera — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimera_of_Buganda))
4. The ancestral **sister** in the migration story is named **Nnandawula** — a female founder
   remembered by name in a patrilineal system. ([buganda.or.ug](https://buganda.or.ug/clan/nseenene-clan/))
5. In Buganda you are never just yourself: to introduce yourself you must name your father, your
   paternal grandfather, your lineage, your clan, its totem, and its motto — identity is genealogy
   spoken aloud. ([BeingAfrican](https://beingafrican.org/culture-panel/clans-and-totems-in-buganda-culture/))

### Unverified / needs Nanda to confirm
- **"Kabombola," "Kyakasuku," "Segoma in Kayenje," "Kayenje–Butambala," "Nsiisi–Busujju"** as the
  specific family lineage seats. *Partially anchored:* **Busujju** and **Butambala** are confirmed
  historic Buganda counties (amasaza), and **Nsiisi in Busujju** appears in Nseenene clan tradition
  as where the ancestor **Kalibbala** settled — so "Nsiisi–Busujju" is consistent with the record.
  Sources: [Buganda Counties](https://buganda.or.ug/counties/),
  [africanculture1](https://africanculture1.blogspot.com/2019/08/nsenene-grasshoper-clan.html).
  **However, "Kabombola," "Kyakasuku," "Segoma," and "Kayenje" could not be verified online** as
  named places or as the family's specific sub-lineage (enju/mutuba). These read as authentic
  family-transmitted lineage detail that simply isn't published — treat as oral-family record, not
  web-verifiable. Ask Nanda / family elders to confirm spellings and which is a place vs. a person.
- The exact family line from the clan founders down to Kabali-Kagwa is family knowledge, not online.

## 2. The Name Kagwa / Kabali-Kagwa — Buganda

### Verified

- **Sir Apolo Kagwa (c. 1869–1927) is a major historical figure of Buganda** — **Katikkiro (prime
  minister)** of the Kingdom of Buganda from **1890 to 1926**, appointed by Kabaka Mwanga II. He
  served as **regent** for the infant Kabaka Daudi Chwa (from 1897 until the king came of age in
  1914) and was a lead negotiator of the **1900 Uganda Agreement** that defined Buganda's status
  under the British protectorate.
  Sources: [Britannica — Sir Apolo Kagwa](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Apolo-Kagwa),
  [Apollo Kaggwa — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Kaggwa)

- **Kagwa was also Buganda's first great ethnographer/author.** He wrote *Basekabaka be Buganda*
  (a history of the kings), *Empisa z'Abaganda* (customs and laws), and *Engero z'Abaganda* (folklore)
  — foundational written records of Ganda history and custom.
  Sources: [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Apolo-Kagwa),
  [Daily Monitor — "Apolo Kaggwa: the colossus of Buganda"](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/special-reports/uganda-50/apolo-kaggwa-the-colossus-of-buganda-1515848)

- **Katikkiro** is the title for the head of government (prime minister) of the Kingdom of Buganda,
  a still-living office within the kingdom today.
  Source: [Katikkiro of Buganda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katikkiro_of_Buganda)

**Evocative, sourced details:**
1. The Kagwa name is attached to the man often called *"the colossus of Buganda"* — a commoner's son
   who rose to run a kingdom and outlast kings. ([Daily Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/special-reports/uganda-50/apolo-kaggwa-the-colossus-of-buganda-1515848))
2. Apolo Kagwa held real power for **36 years** and governed as regent — he "dominated the chiefs in
   the Lukiiko [parliament]." ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Apolo-Kagwa))
3. He was one of the earliest Baganda converts to Protestant Christianity and a leader of that
   faction in the 1888–92 civil wars. ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Apolo-Kagwa))
4. He is the reason much pre-colonial Ganda history survives in writing at all — he wrote it down.
   ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Apolo-Kagwa))

### Unverified / needs Nanda to confirm
- **Whether the family descends from, or is directly related to, Sir Apolo Kagwa.** The web confirms
  the *historical* significance of the Kagwa name and of Apolo Kagwa; it does **not** confirm the
  Kabali-Kagwa family's specific descent from him. Do **not** state or imply direct descent on the
  Roots page unless Nanda confirms it. It is accurate to say the family carries a name **borne by**
  one of Buganda's most consequential statesmen — that is verifiable; a bloodline claim is not.
- The meaning/origin of the compound **"Kabali-Kagwa"** and of **"Kabali"** specifically was not
  found in sources — ask Nanda.

## 3. AmaTshawe (Tshawe) — Founding Royal House of the amaXhosa, South Africa

### Verified

- **Tshawe is the progenitor of Xhosa kingship.** Xhosa oral tradition holds that **all Xhosa
  subgroups descend from one ancestor, Tshawe**, and that Tshawe and his father-line were "probably
  the first Xhosa kings or paramount chiefs." The **amaTshawe** are described as the founding /
  ruling royal house.
  Sources: [South African History Online — Xhosa](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa),
  [List of Xhosa kings — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Xhosa_kings)

- **How Tshawe won the kingship.** By tradition, on the death of their father **Nkosiyamntu**,
  succession was disputed among the brothers: **Cirha** (Great House) and **Jwarha** (Right-Hand
  House) versus **Tshawe** (a junior/minor house, iqadi). Tshawe, aided by warriors from the
  neighbouring **Mpondomise**, defeated Cirha and Jwarha and made his line the ruling house of the
  Xhosa ever after.
  Sources: [SAHO — Xhosa](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa),
  [List of Xhosa kings — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Xhosa_kings)

- **The royal line branches from Tshawe.** Later kings — **Phalo**, and his sons **Gcaleka** and
  **Rharhabe** (founding the two great houses), down to **Hintsa kaKhawuta** — all trace back to
  Tshawe as founding ancestor.
  Sources: [SAHO — Xhosa](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa),
  [Hintsa kaKhawuta — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hintsa_kaKhawuta)

- **Isiduko (clan name) system.** Among the Xhosa, the **isiduko** (clan name) passes patrilineally
  from father to son and is a person's deepest identity marker — more important, culturally, than
  the surname. It situates a person in a lineage of shared ancestry.
  Sources: [SAHO — Xhosa](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa),
  [South African Clans — Xhosa Clan Names](https://southafricanclans.com/xhosa-clan-names/)

- **The imbongi (praise poet).** Xhosa oral tradition centres on the **imbongi** (plural iimbongi),
  the praise singer who lives near the chief's "great place," accompanies the chief on important
  occasions, and recites **izibongo / imibongo** — praises of the deeds of chiefs and ancestors.
  Source: [SAHO — Xhosa](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa)

**Evocative, sourced details:**
1. The amaTshawe are described as **the oldest royal house in South Africa** — Xhosa kingship begins
   with this name. ([List of Xhosa kings — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Xhosa_kings))
2. Tshawe was the *junior* son who took the throne — kingship earned, not merely inherited, and held
   by his line ever since. ([SAHO](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa))
3. Xhosa kingship was never absolutist: the king headed a lineage but junior chiefs kept real
   autonomy — a federation of houses, not a single crown. ([SAHO](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa))
4. Every Xhosa carries an **isiduko**; to know someone's clan name is to know their ancestors and
   to be able to praise them by it. ([SAHO](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa))
5. The **imbongi** keeps the memory of the house alive out loud — history as performed poetry.
   ([SAHO](https://sahistory.org.za/article/xhosa))

### Unverified / needs Nanda to confirm
- **The family's specific Xhosa isiduko and its line back to Tshawe.** The web confirms Tshawe as the
  founding royal ancestor of the amaXhosa *as a nation*; it does not confirm the family's particular
  clan branch or a documented descent chain. If the family's Xhosa isiduko is itself "Tshawe"
  (i.e. of the royal house), that is a strong claim — confirm with Nanda before stating descent.

## 4. AmaHlubi & the Msimango Clan — South Africa

### Verified

- **AmaHlubi are an Nguni people** of southern Africa, today concentrated in KwaZulu-Natal and the
  Eastern Cape, tracing a line of kings back to roughly the 14th century. Their tradition descends
  from **Mnguni** (the abeliNguni), and their kings include **Bhungane** and, most famously,
  **Langalibalele I** (r. 1839–1889).
  Sources: [Uvelaphi — Hlubi people](https://uvelaphi.africa/cultures/hlubi.html),
  [iAfrika — Imbali yesizwe sama Hlubi](https://iafrika.org/xh/imbali-yesizwe-sama-hlubi-clan-names/)

- **The Mfecane shattered and scattered the Hlubi kingdom.** Around 1818, **Matiwane's amaNgwane**
  attacked the Hlubi, killing chief **Mthimkhulu**; survivors fled — north, west, into Shaka's Zulu,
  or into refugee groups such as the **amaFengu (Mfengu)**. This dispersal is why Hlubi-descended
  clans (including Msimango) are found across many provinces and neighbouring countries.
  Sources: [Uvelaphi — Hlubi](https://uvelaphi.africa/cultures/hlubi.html),
  [izithakazelo.blog — Msimanga clan](https://izithakazelo.blog/izithakazelo-zakwamsimanga-msimanga-clan/)

- **The Msimango clan is Hlubi.** The isiThakazelo source states plainly: *"Abantu bakwaMsimanga
  bangamaHlubi"* — the people of Msimanga are amaHlubi. The surname is found across South Africa,
  Botswana, Lesotho and Zimbabwe, spelled Msimango / Msimanga / Msimang by region.
  Source: [izithakazelo.blog — Msimanga clan](https://izithakazelo.blog/izithakazelo-zakwamsimanga-msimanga-clan/)

- **The praise names the family carries are genuine documented Msimango izithakazelo.**
  "Msimango · Thabizolo · Nonkosi · Mlotshwa · Ngelengele" all appear in recorded Msimango clan
  praises. A documented longer version reads:
  > *"Thabizolo, Nonkosi, Mlotshwa, Wena kaSengama umuntu ngejozi, Wena owehla ngesilulu abafokazane
  > behla ngezinyawo, Pembe kaLokothwayo, Ngelengele!"*
  Sources: [zuluring — Msimango](https://zuluring.blogspot.com/2011/05/msimango.html),
  [Ulwazi Programme — Izithakazelo zakwaMsimango](https://www.ulwaziprogramme.org/izithakazelo-zakwamsimango/),
  [izithakazelo.blog](https://izithakazelo.blog/izithakazelo-zakwamsimanga-msimanga-clan/)

- **What izithakazelo are.** Izithakazelo (sing. isithakazelo) are Nguni clan praises — poems that
  honour a clan's ancestors and carry, in compressed poetic form, the story and genealogy of the
  clan. Reciting them is a mark of respect and a way of "placing" a person.
  Source: [Ulwazi Programme](https://www.ulwaziprogramme.org/izithakazelo-zakwamsimango/)

**Evocative, sourced details:**
1. The Msimango are **amaHlubi** — a people old enough to count kings back to the 1300s.
   ([Uvelaphi](https://uvelaphi.africa/cultures/hlubi.html))
2. The clan's very geographic spread — across four countries — is a *fingerprint of the Mfecane*,
   the great scattering; the family's presence in more than one nation echoes that history.
   ([izithakazelo.blog](https://izithakazelo.blog/izithakazelo-zakwamsimanga-msimanga-clan/))
3. One praise line — *"owehla ngesilulu, abafokazane behla ngezinyawo"* ("the one who descends in a
   grain-basket while commoners come down on foot") — is an image of rank and distinction carried in
   the clan's own poetry. ([zuluring](https://zuluring.blogspot.com/2011/05/msimango.html))
4. **Ngelengele** and **Thabizolo** are ancestral praise-names, spoken to greet and honour a
   Msimango. ([Ulwazi Programme](https://www.ulwaziprogramme.org/izithakazelo-zakwamsimango/))
5. The Hlubi language (isiHlubi), close to siSwati, is today **endangered**, with Hlubi intellectuals
   working to preserve it — heritage under active protection. ([Uvelaphi](https://uvelaphi.africa/cultures/hlubi.html))

### Unverified / needs Nanda to confirm
- The **exact meanings** of individual praise names (Thabizolo, Nonkosi, Mlotshwa, Ngelengele) — the
  sources list them as praises but do not gloss each one. Present them as praise-names, not as
  translated words, unless a family elder can gloss them.
- The family's specific place within the Msimango/Hlubi line (which house, which region of origin).

## 5. The Buganda Self-Introduction — Naming Your Line

### Verified

- **Reciting your lineage is a defined cultural practice in Buganda.** In a formal introduction —
  especially at ceremonies such as the **kwanjula (okwanjula)**, the traditional introduction/marriage
  ceremony — a Muganda is expected to be able to state: their surname, their **father's** name, their
  **paternal grandfather's** name, their **mother's clan**, their own **clan**, its **totem**, its
  **motto (omubala)**, and their place in the lineage (akasolya → ssiga → mutuba → lunyiriri →
  nnyumba). Identity is expressed as descent.
  Sources: [BeingAfrican](https://beingafrican.org/culture-panel/clans-and-totems-in-buganda-culture/),
  [Clans of Baganda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clans_of_Baganda),
  [Harusi Hub — Kwanjula guide](https://blog.harusihub.com/post/kwanjula-guide-uganda/)

- **Marriage is understood as a covenant between clans, not just individuals** — which is why the
  kwanjula centres on formally naming and joining two lineages.
  Source: [Harusi Hub — Kwanjula guide](https://blog.harusihub.com/post/kwanjula-guide-uganda/)

**Editorial use:** The family's introduction — *"I come from the line of Kabombola who reigns from
Kyakasuku…"* — is a real *form* (naming your ancestor and his seat is exactly how it's done). The
**form is verified and culturally authentic**; the **specific names** in it (Kabombola, Kyakasuku)
fall under the Unverified note in §1. It is safe and accurate to present the *practice* of
introducing yourself by naming your ancestral line and seat as genuine Buganda custom.

### Unverified / needs Nanda to confirm
- The specific content of the family's introduction (the named ancestor **Kabombola** and seat
  **Kyakasuku**) — authentic-sounding oral-family record, not web-verifiable. Confirm spellings and
  whether Kabombola is an ancestor and Kyakasuku his place.

## Handling note for the Roots page
- Verified clan history, totems, mottoes, praise-poetry and the named historical figures (Kimera,
  Apolo Kagwa, Tshawe, Langalibalele) are safe to build editorial copy around.
- **Do not assert direct personal descent** from Sir Apolo Kagwa, from Kabaka Kimera, or from King
  Tshawe unless Nanda confirms it. It is both accurate and powerful to say the family *carries the
  names and clans of* these houses — which is verifiable — without claiming a documented bloodline.
- The family-specific place/ancestor names (Kabombola, Kyakasuku, Segoma, Kayenje) are treated here
  as trusted oral-family record; keep them, but attribute them to family memory, not to a source.
