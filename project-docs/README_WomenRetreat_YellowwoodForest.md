## 🎯 The Problem

Wellness retreats targeting women in South Africa operate in a trust-first market. Before a woman will pay for a retreat experience — especially one set in a forest location — the digital presence has to do serious emotional work. The brief was clear: create something that makes a visitor *feel* the retreat before she even books. Most event websites fail at this. They list dates and prices. This needed to be an experience.

---

## 💡 The Solution

A cinematic, single-page web experience that leads with atmosphere over information. The design philosophy: forest first, form later. Visitors arrive into visual immersion — deep greens, textured backgrounds, unhurried typography — before ever encountering a CTA. The content architecture mirrors the emotional arc of attending a retreat: arrival → surrender → transformation → return.

## ✨ Key Features

- **Cinematic hero section** — full-bleed nature imagery with layered typography
- **Emotional storytelling layout** — content structured as a narrative arc, not a brochure
- **Retreat schedule section** — visually designed day-by-day flow
- **Facilitator profile** — trust-building human element above the fold
- **Booking CTA** — appears after emotional investment is established, not before
- **Mobile-first responsive** — fully optimised for the smartphone-first SA market
- **Accessibility** — semantic HTML, readable contrast ratios, focus states

---

## 📐 Design Decisions

**Why no JavaScript framework?** The site needed to load instantly and be maintainable by a non-technical client. Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS with zero dependencies means zero build complexity and maximum performance on mobile data in South Africa.

**Why a narrative layout over a standard event page?** Women's wellness retreats sell transformation, not logistics. The layout was structured to answer *"Why should I trust this?"* before it answered *"When and where?"*

## 🌱 Build Journey

This project was an early test of a principle that has shaped everything built since: **design is not decoration, it is communication**. The challenge was resisting the urge to show off technically and instead serving the one question the visitor is asking: *"Is this safe for me?"*

Working with nature photography and forest imagery also introduced the discipline of designing around content you don't control — the colours, compositions, and textures of the location photography had to dictate the design system, not the other way around.

The project became the foundation for how client landing pages are approached across the Mirembe Muse portfolio: start with the emotional destination, work backwards to the technical implementation.
