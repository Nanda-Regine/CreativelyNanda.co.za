The posts were fine. That was the problem.

In August my marketing engine, the part of JarvisOS that writes and designs for each of my brands, prepared a week of posts for Sanyu Botanicals: twelve posts, twice a day, all rendered, all passing every check. The copy was clean. The layouts held. The images were pleasant pictures of hibiscus petals and castor seeds.

I looked at them and knew we could do so much better. Sanyu is a hair and scalp care brand made by hand, with botanicals, in small batches. None of that was on the screen. What was on the screen could have belonged to anyone.

I wrote back fast, the way you do when you already know. The vault had everything, I said. There were proper videos in there. Look at everything in that vault.

:::chapter
I | What the index said
:::

The engine had not been careless in the ordinary sense. It had done what it was built to do. It asked the database which images existed for Sanyu, and the database answered. The answer was a handful of generic botanical stock photographs, because those were the only ones anyone had ever registered in the index.

The folder on disk was another country entirely.

:::figures
95 | photographs
22 | videos
733 MB | in the vault
2 | folders never opened
:::

The index had never been told about any of it. So as far as the system was concerned, it did not exist. The journal entry the agent wrote afterwards names the mistake in one line, and it is the most useful sentence I have read this year about working with AI:

:::pull The JarvisOS build journal, 13 August 2026
The index was not the territory.
:::

:::chapter
II | Looking at every frame
:::

This time the agent opened everything. It built contact sheets, the way photographers used to lay out a roll of film on a light box, and went through all one hundred and seventeen files frame by frame. What was in the vault was not stock. It was the brand.

:::frames From the vault, contact sheet

02 | The gold-lidded balm jar, held in a hand
03 | Botanicals going into the steel bowl

05 | Jars being filled, one by one

09 | Locs threaded with cowrie shells

12 | Twenty-one video clips, the whisking ones already perfect as they are
:::

Frame four is the one. A whisk, lifting thick green paste out of a steel bowl, slowly, in a kitchen. It is not a beautiful product shot. It is better than that. It is proof.

:::chapter
III | What nobody else can post
:::

We looked at the brands Sanyu sits beside, the South African and African natural hair care makers, and the global ones working with chebe. They are good. They lead with their founders' stories and with lists of what their products are free from, and they show their finished products beautifully.

Not one of them showed the making.

A brand whose product is made under contract by someone else cannot post the pot, the whisk, the paste and the jars being filled, because it does not have those pictures. Sanyu does. It had them the whole time, in a folder with a slightly awkward transfer-service name that nobody had opened. The thing that made the brand impossible to copy was sitting one directory away from the stock photos we had used instead.

:::statement
The unfair advantage was in the vault. We had marketed around it.
:::

So the week was rebuilt from the vault. The first post became the whisk and the paste, with a line of four words: *Before it was a balm.* After it came the branded bottle on marble, the locs and cowries, the botanicals going into the bowl, the balm held outdoors, and the whole line-up under palm leaves. Every post now carries a way to reach the shop, where before only two of the twelve had. The six stock-image posts were retired, not deleted, and nothing that had been scheduled had gone out yet, so nothing was lost but a week of the wrong idea.

:::chapter
IV | The label and the store
:::

Looking closely turns things up that you did not go looking for.

When the final post rendered with the real bottle in it, the label was legible. It reads *Hair Growth Oil*. The copy, following the product catalogue, called it *Scalp Oil*. On a picture where the customer can read the label, that is a contradiction sitting in plain sight, so the copy now follows the bottle. The shop still uses the other name. One of them will have to change, and that is my decision to make, not the engine's. But I would never have known if the picture had been a hibiscus petal.

:::chapter
V | Knowing about, and looking at
:::

I think about this week often, because it names the most important thing I have learned about building with AI.

The system did not lie. It reported, accurately, everything it had been told. What it could not do was notice that what it had been told was a small and dusty fraction of what was true. A database is a record of what someone once decided to write down. It is not the world. The world is on disk, in the camera roll, in the kitchen with the steel bowl, and something has to go and look at it.

As a poet I have always known this. You do not write the rose from the word *rose*. You write it from the one on the table, with the brown edge on the third petal that nobody else would put in the poem. Engineering, it turns out, is the same discipline under a different name. A list of what exists is not the same as having seen it.

:::pull
Before it was a balm.
:::

What I ask for now, before any campaign, is the contact sheet. And the first post of that rebuilt week is the one I keep coming back to: four words, a whisk, and a bowl nobody else could have photographed.
